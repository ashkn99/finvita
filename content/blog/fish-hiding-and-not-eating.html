Why is my fish hiding and not eating?

Fish often show behavioral changes before any physical symptom shows up. A water quality problem that hasn't produced a visible sign yet is the most common cause, but recent changes — a new tankmate, rearranged decor, a move to a new tank — can also cause temporary stress on their own. Less commonly, it's the early stage of an illness that hasn't developed external symptoms like spots or fin damage yet.

Should I be worried right away?

A day of reduced appetite after a stressful event, like adding new fish or doing a big water change, usually isn't a concern on its own. It's worth acting on when hiding and appetite loss persist for several days, or when other symptoms start appearing alongside it.

What to do

  1. Test your water first — ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH, and temperature.
  2. Watch closely for any developing symptoms (spots, discoloration, clamped fins) over the next few days.
  3. Reduce other stressors: check tankmate compatibility, avoid unnecessary decor changes, and keep feeding consistent.
  4. Consider a quarantine tank if you suspect illness, so you can observe closely without risking the rest of the tank.

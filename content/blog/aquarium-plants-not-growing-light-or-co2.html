Aquarium plants that stay small or barely grow are almost always light- or CO2-limited, not diseased. A basic starter light often doesn't provide enough intensity or duration for common live plants, and a dedicated full-spectrum plant light is the most common fix.

Why aren't my aquarium plants growing?

Plants need enough light to photosynthesize, and enough CO2 to actually use that light. Most tanks ship with a basic light meant to just illuminate the water, not grow plants — it's not intense enough or doesn't run long enough. Even with a good light, low CO2 availability caps how much a plant can grow, since light and CO2 both have to be adequate for growth to speed up.

How do I tell if it's light or CO2?

If your light is weak, old, or a basic stock fixture, start there — it's the more common and cheaper problem to fix. If you already run a dedicated full-spectrum plant light for 8–10 hours a day and growth is still stalled, CO2 is the more likely bottleneck, especially with faster-growing or "high-tech" species like stem plants or carpeting plants.

Fixing slow or stalled plant growth

  1. Upgrade to a dedicated full-spectrum plant light if you're running a basic stock fixture.
  2. Run the light for a consistent 8–10 hours a day — inconsistent lighting slows growth as much as low intensity does.
  3. Add a liquid CO2 supplement if growth stays slow after improving the light.
  4. Or switch to lower-light, undemanding species like anubias, java fern, or java moss if you'd rather not add more equipment.

How do I know if it's a nutrient problem instead?

Light and CO2 issues show up as slow or stunted growth overall. If growth is fine but leaves are yellowing, developing holes, or growing algae on them, that's a nutrient deficiency or imbalance rather than a light/CO2 problem — see the yellowing leaves guide or holes in leaves guide.

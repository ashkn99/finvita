Ragged, frayed, or steadily disappearing fin edges — sometimes with a white or red tinge — usually point to fin rot, a bacterial infection that takes hold when water quality is poor or a fish is stressed. Treating the infection while also improving water quality gives noticeably better results than either one alone.

Will fin rot fins grow back?

Yes, in most cases. Once the infection is treated and water quality is stable, fins regrow over the following weeks. The regrown edge sometimes looks slightly different in color or shape than the original, especially after a severe case, but function returns to normal.

What causes fin rot?

  • Poor water quality — elevated ammonia, nitrite, or nitrate — is the most common trigger, since it stresses fish and lets opportunistic bacteria take hold on already-weakened fin tissue.
  • Physical injury from aggressive tankmates, sharp decor, or fin-nipping species creates an entry point for infection.
  • Overcrowding and low oxygen add ongoing stress that makes fish generally more susceptible to any infection, fin rot included.

How to treat fin rot

  1. Test your water and do a partial water change to correct any ammonia, nitrite, or nitrate issue — this addresses the root cause, not just the symptom.
  2. Treat with a fin rot / bacterial medication for the infection itself.
  3. Remove or separate any aggressive tankmates that may be causing ongoing physical damage.
  4. Keep up regular water changes throughout treatment — clean water measurably speeds recovery and helps prevent reinfection.

Can fin rot kill a fish?

Left untreated, it can. Fin rot that isn't caught early can progress from the fins into the fish's body, which is significantly more dangerous and harder to treat. That's the main reason it's worth treating as soon as you notice ragged or receding fin edges rather than waiting to see if it resolves on its own.

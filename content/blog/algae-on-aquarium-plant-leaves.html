Algae growing directly on plant leaves points to a light/nutrient imbalance — usually too much light for the nutrients available, which lets algae outcompete the plant for what's there. Cutting back light duration alongside an algae treatment usually clears it within a week or two.

Why is algae growing on my plant leaves?

Algae and plants compete for the same light and nutrients. When light is excessive relative to the nutrients in the water, or when plants are growing slowly and not using up what's available, algae fills the gap and colonizes leaf surfaces directly. Long light durations — more than 8–10 hours a day — accelerate this on any surface, including leaves.

Is it hurting my plants?

A light dusting usually isn't fatal on its own, but a heavy coating blocks light from reaching the leaf and can smother slower-growing plants over time. It's worth treating even mild cases before they spread, since algae on leaves tends to get worse rather than resolve on its own.

How to remove algae from plant leaves

  1. Reduce light duration to 6–8 hours a day.
  2. Treat with an algae control product to clear the current growth.
  3. Keep plants well-fertilized so they grow fast enough to outcompete algae for nutrients going forward.
  4. Manually remove heavily affected leaves if algae doesn't clear after treatment.

Yellowing leaves on aquarium plants usually mean a nitrogen or iron deficiency, most common in tanks that aren't fertilized on a regular schedule. A liquid plant fertilizer typically resolves it within a couple of weeks by replacing what the water column alone doesn't provide.

Why are my aquarium plant leaves turning yellow?

Live plants pull nitrogen and iron directly from the water column. In a lightly-planted or unfertilized tank, fish waste alone often isn't enough to keep up, especially once plants are established and growing quickly. The result is yellowing that typically starts with older leaves as the plant reallocates the nutrients it has toward newer growth.

Is this always a deficiency?

Not always. A single old leaf near the base of the plant yellowing and dropping is often just normal leaf turnover — the plant shedding older growth as it focuses energy on newer leaves. Widespread yellowing across the plant, especially on new growth, is the stronger signal of an actual nutrient deficiency worth correcting.

How to fix yellowing leaves

  1. Add a liquid plant fertilizer on a regular schedule — check the product for dosing frequency, since consistency matters more than any single dose.
  2. Trim off badly yellowed leaves so the plant can redirect its energy toward new, healthy growth.
  3. Check whether it's isolated to older leaves (often normal) or affecting new growth too (more likely a genuine deficiency).
  4. For heavy root-feeders like swords or crypts, add root tabs — liquid fertilizer alone doesn't always reach the substrate where these species feed.

How often should you fertilize?

Most liquid fertilizers are dosed a few times a week rather than daily or monthly — check the specific product's label, since concentration varies. The more heavily planted or fast-growing your tank, the more consistently it needs fertilizing to keep up with what the plants are actually using.

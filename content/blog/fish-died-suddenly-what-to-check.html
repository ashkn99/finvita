In an established, stable tank, occasional fish loss can happen from old age or causes that aren't always preventable. Still, test your water before adding any new fish — a hidden water quality issue can affect the rest of your tank even if only one fish showed symptoms.

Why did my fish die suddenly?

In a tank that's otherwise been stable, sudden loss often comes down to one of three things: natural causes like old age (especially in short-lived species), a water quality issue that hadn't produced an obvious symptom yet, or underlying stress or disease that wasn't caught in time. It's not always possible to pin down which one after the fact, which is why the next step matters more than the diagnosis.

What should I check before adding new fish?

Test your water even if the rest of the tank looks completely normal. A water quality problem can be present without every fish showing symptoms, and adding new, more sensitive fish into an already-stressed tank is a common way to lose a second one shortly after the first.

What to do after a fish dies

  1. Test your water before adding any new fish, even if the rest of the tank looks fine.
  2. Remove the deceased fish promptly to prevent it from affecting water quality as it decomposes.
  3. Watch remaining fish closely for a week or two for any delayed symptoms.
  4. Avoid restocking immediately — give it a few days to confirm the rest of the tank is stable.
